What started as a simple RoF test spiralled out of control into the discussion of the firing modes in Insurgency Sandstorm. Hopefully, you find some of it informative.

Testing

In-game stats the Rate of Fire (RoF) of the M16A2 is 296 RPM and the M16A4 is 316RPM. Just looking at the values they appear a bit slow and was curious to see where that value comes from. I’ll show the result upfront first then explain.

Rate of Fire Formula

Given:

  • (S) = salvo length (shots)
  • (R) = time between consecutive shots (seconds)
  • (B) = additional delay after a salvo (seconds)

Rate of Fire per Minute (60 seconds)

\[\text{RPM} = \frac{60 \times S} {((S - 1) \times R) + B}\]

Summary of testing results

Calculating (Table) the theoretical values based on the in-game stats without testing. I included the G3 on auto as a control, and it behaved as expected with no errors when I tested it.

Name Stats RPM 30 round time (s) Bullets per second 1 Bullet time (s)
M16A2 296 6.08 4.93 0.20
M16A4 316 5.69 5.26 0.18
G36K 741 2.42 12.35 0.08
FAMAS 1000 1.80 16.66 0.06
G3 500 3.60 8.33 0.12

As you can see (Table) the calculated results doesn’t match the displayed game stats, why?

M16A2 450 4.00 7.50 0.133 0.2 296 0.986 608
M16A4 490 3.67 8.17 0.122 0.18 316 0.88 575
G36K 575 3.13 9.58 0.104 0.127 469 0.08 741
FAMAS 740 2.43 12.34 0.081 0.173 346 0.06 1000

The 30-round time is from the recording of tests. From there it is easy to calculate the overall RoF. I tried to discover both the shot and delay RoF from the recordings too but was not happy with the result as I think frame rounding could skew the results.

When I initially tested the M16A2 it seems like the Shot RoF was about 600 and the delay was 300 RPM. I was happy to discover that the ‘300’ is extremely close to the in-game stat RoF (296) for the M16. So I think that the in-game value for the M16A2/4 is for the burst delay. By knowing the Overall RoF, and the delay RoF I was able to calculate the Shot RoF too.

For both M16A2 & M16A4, the actual RoF is higher than what is stated in-game which is an interesting finding. I think these calculated values seem accurate. For the G36K / FAMAS, I am less certain about my calculated results. Both weapons have a higher Automatic RoF than Burst RoF recorded, which is not surprising as a burst delay will just make the elapsed time longer, lowering the overall RoF.

Update

After this was posted I found a good source (insurgencytracker.com) of information, and learnt that the in-game displayed 296 RPM uses NWI’s convention of showing 40% of the cyclic rate for burst/semi-only weapons (741 × 0.4 ≈ 296).

Rate of Fire Testing comparison video

Please note some of the limitations of my testing from user input, frame rounding from recording and math calculated from data which make contain some errors and can lead to inaccuracy.

I don’t use any scripts, macros, mods etc to modify my click rate. My guess is each weapon has a limiter on its Semi-Auto fire rate. This was my attempt at clicking as fast as possible for 30 shots. I think the limiting factor here is me, so consider that. The World Record for Clicks per second on this site is 17.4, meaning when converted to Fire rate it would be 1,044RPM! My Semi-Auto RoF (557RPM) attempt is higher than the stated game stats (296RPM), the limit should be 741 RPM for the M16A2.

Firing modes Discussion

Firing Mode Specific

How it works (Game mechanics)

Automatic will keep shooting while the trigger is pressed at an exact fire rate. Main Balancing factors in games include fire rate, damage and recoil. Playing around with these variables you get different styles of weapons. Metrics used are often Time to Kill (TTK) or Shots to Kill (STK).

Semi-Auto only shoots 1 bullet at a time with each trigger press. Practically is you who determines the fire rate in this mode. Games tend to have limiters in case people try to exploit this mechanic. Semi-Auto weapons can be difficult to balance as they are often referred to as having a high skill ceiling and depending on how they are implemented by the game mechanics they can be trash or overpowered.

Burst will shoot a specific number of bullets at a set fire rate which will have a delay before it can be shot again. To shoot again you need to press the trigger again when the weapon is ready. Burst Weapons can be hit or miss in how they are implemented in games. Its own distinction is how practical the spread of the shots in the burst and how long the burst delay is.

What is the point of the burst mode?

The tip in the game is “Use [button] to change a weapon’s fire mode if has more than one. Semi-Auto fire is effective at longer ranger where full auto is harder to control.”

If a generalisation for the mode is; Semi-Auto is for precision and Automatic is for suppression. What is the point of burst and why does it exist? From my understanding, the main points are:

  • As an intermediate mode between Semi and Auto.
  • To not waste ammo (panicked full auto mag dump)
  • Recoil control (X bullets is enough for the target)

Typical Engagement ranges in-game.

I think for the insurgency maps, there is a lot of medium-range combat, for the objectives themselves are located in close quarters.

Some rough thresholds:

  • <25m Close quarters
  • 25-50m: Short
  • 50-100m: Medium
  • 100m+: Long

When you play the tutorial ‘range’ level, the enemies that come over the ridge are about 50m away from the balcony. You can modify your playstyle to select engagements that you are comfortable with.

Ease of Use in Close quarters

  • Full auto: is the easiest to use as you just “fire” when on target and don’t when not on target. All you need to do is focus on recoil control and tracking the target.
  • Burst is slightly harder than full auto as it has a delay between bursts which means you must take a bit more care in your initial aim before you fire.
  • Single-shot is the hardest, as you control the fire rate. Even if you can spam the fire button at an insanely high rate can you do it precisely too? And can you do it consistently? For example, in the Semi-Auto clip kills 8&9 were at a fire rate of about 375RPM (Slower than all automatic rifles).

Other Factors to consider

Fire rate

I don’t consider fire rate that important in INS: SS. Mainly because the “Shot To Kill” (STK) is very low (2-3). If the STK was higher like 5-7, I would consider the fire rate more important since If more hits are required this extends the elapsed time (even with 100% accuracy) and a higher fire rate will also help compensate for inaccuracy too.

For Example, if an AKM and FAMAS kill you in 2 shots, and AKM shoots every 0.1s and the FAMAS every 0.06s. If you both had 100% accuracy and shot at the exact same time (1st shot: 0s), the FAMAS will win by 0.04s with the 2nd shot. For a 60FPS user that is only 2.5 frames difference. I would rate positioning, awareness, and accuracy as more important.

Loadout Factors

I often see “What is the best weapon?” I would say in INS:SS all rifleman choices are viable, and the distinctions are more to do with player preferences. I don’t think there are “OP” weapons like in other games. When discussing “Best” there any many often factors left out. In short, I don’t think there is a “best” unless you define the context of “best” first.

  • Are supply points considered?
    • If you are making an equipment-heavy loadout, you cannot spend as many points on your primary weapon, however, if you are making a very light loadout you can spend more, so the difference in supply points shifts the focus.
  • Attachments?
    • Are certain attachments important to you? For example, FAL, G3, and QBZ-03 cannot equip a smoke/explosive launcher does this matter for your loadout?
  • Specific maps?
    • The impact of maps is not often discussed. Typical engagement ranges in “Crossing” is very different to “Prison”. Certain attributes of a weapon may be more suited for specific map scenarios.
  • Game mode?
    • “Push” is different to “Ambush”. And to split it further, being an “attacker” is different to a “defender” in push.
  • Playstyle focus?
    • Certain tools are better suited for specific jobs. Even stating “versatile” hints toward a specification.

In IN:SS you have 15 supply points to spend on a loadout in the game mode of PvP Push. Primary weapons range from 1 to 6 points. I have multiple load-outs for different scenarios. I choose both [PvP] and [as the attacking teams] as it helps constrain the scope and has interesting points to consider.

For me, the gas mask is essential not just for chemical mortars but allows for not making a coughing sound when moving through the smoke. Smoke is important for creating concealment so you can reach the objective. I personally like the smoke launcher as it is both cost-effective and keeps my equipment slots free. I like to have at least 1 explosive to assist in clearing the objective. I like having a 4x-1x BUS optic as it keeps the weapon versatile for both long-range and short-range. A flash hider is important, so you don’t reveal your location visually when firing.

Weapon Specific

M16A2 [1pt]

Objectively the M16A2 is the worst out of all the burst weapons due longest burst delay (0.2s). However, its main draw is that it is the cheapest primary weapon at 1 point, which is great as it allows to spend lots of points on equipment and attachments. Also, the main stats are very good, with a very high bullet velocity and low recoil. M16A2 is one of my favourite weapons in the game and it looks cool when it has that style of M203 grenade launcher attached. The delay isn’t too long, and if you don’t like it, you can switch it to semi so there is no delay, to overcome this. Naturally a lower RoF will be at a disadvantage against higher RoF weapon in a close quarters environment by comparison. An minor detail which is a nice bonus is the M16A2 uses STANAG magazines which means it can take the magazines from compatible weapons or if you swap weapons you can use your own reserve ammo. Weapons that share the same 5.56mm NATO magazines are: M16A2, M16A4, M4A1, Mk 18 CBR, L85A2, and QBZ-03.

M16A4 [2pts]

Extremely similar to the M16A2, differences include costing 1 supply point extra and more variety in attachments (Optics), and slightly better fire rate. If you are looking to make the cheapest Security weapon with a 4x-1x optic the QTS-11 may be a better alternative even though the weapon itself is 4pts but the unique optic is only 1 point, and the weapon is full auto (550 RPM) for people who dislike the burst mode. Also since the M16A4 is 2 points, for 1 point extra you can access the G36K / AUG, so often people just use them instead.

G36K [3pts]

You can start off with G36K vs AUG debate. I have a sight personal preference for the AUG over the G36K for very minor reasons. Firstly I prefer Bullet velocity over rate of fire (main distinction between them). I like only having 2 firing modes for simplicity, It means I can toggle between Auto & Semi instead of cycling between Auto, Burst, Semi. Being a Bullpup is a nice bonus so you are less likely to see a barrel poking round a corner. However you can argue that bullet velocity is not as important as how often do you target enemies 150m+, a higher RoF is better in close quarters, and the 2-round burst adds to the versatility. Overall I think both are good. Commenting specifically on the 2-Round burst aspect of the G36K. Initially I was unsure what to make of it, but I like it. It has the shortest burst delay out all the burst weapons so using the burst plays more like Semi-Auto as the delay is minimal. Having 2 bullets instead of 1 makes it feel more consistent. A single burst of 2 shots can kill a enemy. The 2 shot bursts allows you have a slower trigger finger yet keep a higher overall fire rate. The 2-round burst is a good intermediate choice between selecting auto and semi. You can use the burst cover that ranges you don’t like to use full auto but want more suppressing fire than semi.

FAMAS [6pts]

Statistically, the FAMAS can be considered one the best weapons in the game due to Its full auto fire rate is extremely high at 1000 RPM (Joint 2nd in the game) with the next highest rifle being the VHS-2 at 857RPM, which means it can melt you faster then they can kill you. The weapon does have major drawbacks. It is the most expensive primary weapon and It only has 25 capacity magazine with no option to extend it. You can empty a 25-round magazine in 1.5s in full auto. If you intend to use full auto you need to have good trigger discipline. If you limit yourself to only use semi, you are wasting points as its primary advantage is the fire rate. An M16A2 would do the job better as it has better bullet velocity, better recoil, a larger magazine, and a much cheaper supply cost. So what about the 3 round burst for the FAMAS? The delay on the burst delay is shortest out of the 3 round burst weapons (M16A2/A4) and the shot delay is the shortest too. This makes it feel very sharp to use. The burst is good as you can mentally know you have 8 bursts (24 bullets) per magazine, meaning even if you double burst each enemy, you can still eliminate 4 enemies per magazine, which is acceptable. The gun itself is fun to use, feels like you are zapping them in each burst. However, being pragmatic, I struggle to justify picking it for my loadout. Considering it is the most expensive rifle it reduces my equipment/ attachment options. My performance with it is not exceptionally better than if I was using a different weapon. In the same scenarios, I think I could do the same job with AK-74 or M16A2. So, my main issue is not with the weapon itself but more in the context of how it fits into my playstyle for the game.
